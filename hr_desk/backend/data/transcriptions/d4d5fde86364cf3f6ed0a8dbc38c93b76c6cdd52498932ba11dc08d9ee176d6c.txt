Incident Report Guidelines. Dated. Prepared by. Designation. Employee number. Department. Location. General Guidelines to Prepare an Incident Report. Operational, or incident, reports describe non-typical events noticed during daily duties, such as criminal offences or unusual activities. The report must contain a clear and accurate description of what transpired. Its purposes are: to become an official record of what happened; to record the occurrence for the writer's future reference; to pass the facts of an occurrence on to others for various uses. Essential Ingredients of Good Operational Reporting. Organized. Gather and record facts promptly. Plan the report in advance and present information in chronological order. Clear. Keep sentences and paragraphs concise and to the point. Legible. Ensure handwriting, if used, is clear; otherwise use bold capitals. Complete. Address all relevant questions: who, what, where, when, why, and how. Accurate. Report only verified facts; avoid assumptions. Report Should Answer the Following Questions. Who was involved? Who was the complainant? Who witnessed the event? Who was the accused or suspect? What actually happened? What evidence is available? When did the event occur? Where did the event occur? Describe the environment and positions of individuals. Why did it happen? What motive was there for the incident? Why did this series of events occur? How did this event come to your attention? How did the event take place? How did the accused act? How did the witnesses act? How was the evidence recovered? How was the suspect or accused arrested?
